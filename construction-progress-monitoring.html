What It Is

One Project Record Built Across Time.

Construction progress monitoring and documentation combines recurring aerial flights and 360° ground capture into a time-stamped record of how a project changes from preconstruction through closeout.

Instead of relying on scattered cell phone photos and disconnected folders, orthomosaics, 3D models, aerial imagery, and 360° walkthroughs can be organized around the project and the date they were captured. The result is a visual history that teams can return to as the work advances.

Scope Matters

Visual Record or Measured Deliverable. Define It Up Front.

Not every construction-progress capture is intended for survey-grade measurement. Many assignments are designed primarily to create a consistent visual record for review, reporting, and coordination.

When a visit must also support a measurable surface, topographic survey, earthwork analysis, or other survey-grade deliverable, Eagle can establish the required survey control and scope the collection accordingly.

The project scope identifies which products are intended for viewing and which are intended for measurement.

Frequently Asked Questions

Construction Progress Monitoring FAQs

Construction progress monitoring and documentation uses recurring aerial flights, orthomosaics, 3D models, and 360° ground capture to create a consistent, time-stamped visual record from preconstruction through closeout. The information helps project executives, superintendents, VDC teams, owners, architects, engineers, and contractors review current conditions, compare work over time, and coordinate without relying on scattered photographs.

The capture frequency is established around the project schedule and the decisions the documentation needs to support. Common intervals include weekly, biweekly, monthly, milestone-based, or before work is concealed. Eagle Surveying can establish repeat flight paths and 360° capture routes so each visit provides a consistent basis for comparison. Weather, airspace, site access, safety, and active operations may affect the capture date.

Depending on the scope, construction progress documentation may include time-stamped aerial imagery, georeferenced orthomosaics, 3D site models, 360° virtual walkthroughs, comparison views, progress timelines, and measurable surfaces tied to survey control. Visual documentation and survey-grade measurement are different deliverables, so Eagle Surveying identifies which portions of the capture are intended for viewing and which are intended for measurement.

Time-stamped imagery and recurring site records can support project communication, RFI review, progress review, pay-application documentation, and dispute evaluation by showing conditions captured on specific dates. The documentation does not independently approve work, certify a pay application, or determine contractual responsibility. Those decisions remain with the owner, contractor, design professional, or other authorized project party.
