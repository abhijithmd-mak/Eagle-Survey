Aerial LiDAR Mapping

Drone LiDAR surveying uses a LiDAR sensor mounted on an unmanned aerial vehicle to emit laser pulses and measure their return times. The measurements build a dense, georeferenced 3D point cloud — and because some pulses reach the ground through openings in vegetation, LiDAR supports bare-earth terrain modeling on wooded sites where photogrammetry may not capture the ground surface.

How it works

Hundreds of thousands of pulses per second, classified into a survey product.

Aerial LiDAR mapping uses a drone-mounted LiDAR sensor that fires hundreds of thousands of pulses per second toward the ground, recording multiple returns per pulse to build a dense, georeferenced 3D point cloud. Because the laser finds gaps in the tree canopy, aerial LiDAR records the bare-earth surface beneath vegetation, something conventional image-based photogrammetry generally cannot accomplish in vegetated areas. Eagle Surveying delivers classified point clouds (ground, vegetation, structures) in LAS/LAZ, digital terrain models (DTM), digital surface models (DSM), contours at your specified interval, and breaklines. We tie every dataset to survey-grade control established with Trimble GNSS receivers, so the deliverable stands up as a survey product, not just a picture.

Eagle operates VTOL (vertical takeoff and landing) fixed-wing and quad UAS platforms, including NDAA-compliant and Blue UAS Cleared platforms suitable for many federal, security-conscious, and critical infrastructure projects. As a survey-first firm with a dedicated Reality Capture practice led by a Director of Reality Capture, we scope point density and accuracy to the engineering use, then process and deliver the data in the coordinate system and format your design team already works in. Because Eagle Surveying is a professional land surveying firm, each dataset is tied to established survey control and reviewed under the oversight of a Texas Registered Professional Land Surveyor.

Aerial LiDAR FAQs

What engineers ask before a LiDAR flight.

Can LiDAR see through trees?+

LiDAR does not literally pass through solid vegetation. Instead, a portion of the laser pulses can reach the ground through gaps in the tree canopy and return ground measurements to the sensor. During processing, those returns can be classified to separate ground, vegetation, and structures. This makes aerial LiDAR particularly useful for topographic mapping, drainage analysis, and site design in wooded or heavily vegetated areas.

How accurate is drone LiDAR?+

Drone LiDAR can achieve survey-grade results when the mission is properly planned, tied to suitable survey control, processed correctly, and scoped to the intended use. Actual accuracy depends on the sensor, flight altitude, control network, calibration, terrain, vegetation, weather, and processing workflow. Eagle Surveying establishes the required accuracy and quality-control approach for each project rather than applying one blanket accuracy claim to every site.

What is bare-earth LiDAR mapping?+

Bare-earth mapping uses classified LiDAR ground returns to create a digital terrain model that represents the ground surface with vegetation and above-ground objects removed from the terrain model. It is commonly used for civil site design, drainage and floodplain analysis, roadway and utility planning, large development tracts, and the wooded properties common across North and Central Texas where the underlying ground cannot be reliably mapped from aerial imagery alone.

Can aerial LiDAR support roadway, rail, pipeline, and transmission line surveys?+

Yes. Aerial LiDAR can efficiently document long linear project areas for roadway, rail, pipeline, power transmission, and distribution work. Typical uses include terrain mapping, vegetation and encroachment documentation, existing-condition mapping, right-of-way and corridor planning, and design-base data. Eagle Surveying's Reality Capture team includes professionals with prior railroad, transit, power transmission, and distribution experience, and those capabilities are available through Eagle for appropriate projects.

Can drone LiDAR support data center and industrial site surveys?+

Yes. Drone LiDAR can support data center site surveys and industrial site surveys across Texas by providing high-density terrain data for due diligence, grading, drainage, access, utility planning, and design-base mapping. On active or developed facilities, LiDAR may be combined with conventional surveying, aerial photogrammetry, or terrestrial laser scanning so the project team receives the appropriate mix of site, structure, and surface information.
