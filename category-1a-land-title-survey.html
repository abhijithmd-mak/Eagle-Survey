What it is

A Texas title survey, under the Manual of Practice.

A Category 1A Land Title Survey is a Texas-specific land title survey performed in accordance with the current Texas Society of Professional Surveyors Manual of Practice. It provides a comprehensive examination and report of significant matters affecting a property’s boundary lines, rights-of-way, easements, and visible improvements.

The survey is generally prepared using the most recent available title commitment or title report, applicable source documents, deed and plat research, field evidence, and professional boundary analysis. The resulting signed and sealed survey provides information that title companies, lenders, buyers, sellers, attorneys, and development teams use during a Texas real estate transaction.

What is a Category 1A Land Title Survey?+

It is a Texas-specific land title survey performed in accordance with the current Texas Society of Professional Surveyors Manual of Practice. It provides a comprehensive examination and report of significant matters affecting a property’s boundary lines, rights-of-way, easements, and visible improvements.

How is it different from an ALTA/NSPS Land Title Survey?+

An ALTA/NSPS survey follows the national Minimum Standard Detail Requirements adopted by ALTA and NSPS and is common in commercial and institutional transactions. A Category 1A survey follows the Texas Society of Professional Surveyors Manual of Practice. The transaction documents, lender, or title company usually specify which one is required.

What does Eagle need to begin?+

The survey is generally prepared using the most recent available title commitment or title report, applicable source documents, and deed and plat research, together with field evidence and professional boundary analysis.

How is a Category 1B different?+

A Category 1B Standard Land Survey, commonly known as a boundary survey, determines and documents property lines without the additional title-related reporting associated with a Category 1A or ALTA/NSPS survey.
