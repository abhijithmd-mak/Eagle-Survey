Aerial Photogrammetry

Drone photogrammetry uses overlapping high-resolution aerial photographs to reconstruct measurable site geometry. The processed outputs may include georeferenced orthomosaics, digital surface models, digital terrain models where conditions allow, RGB point clouds, contours, and 3D photogrammetric models.

The method

See it and measure it, from the same flight.

Aerial photogrammetry reconstructs 3D geometry from hundreds or thousands of overlapping high-resolution photographs captured from a UAS. The output is a survey-accurate, measurable record of the site: georeferenced orthomosaics, digital terrain models (DTM), digital surface models (DSM), RGB point clouds, 3D photogrammetric models, and contours. Where LiDAR excels at penetrating vegetation, photogrammetry excels at visual detail and color, making it the ideal tool for open ground, stockpiles, surface features, and projects where clients need to both see and measure existing conditions.

Eagle Surveying operates VTOL and quad UAS platforms with survey-grade Trimble GNSS receivers and ground control points, then processes imagery in DroneDeploy. Every product is tied to survey control established by our field crew and reviewed by our office staff, so the accuracy is documented, not assumed.

One flight produces the base map, the surface model and the visual record — all in the same coordinate system.
LiDAR or photogrammetry

Two methods, one question: can the camera see the ground?

LiDAR measures distance with laser pulses and is particularly effective for terrain mapping, vertical detail, and ground capture through openings in vegetation. Photogrammetry reconstructs surfaces from overlapping imagery and provides detailed color and visual information. Eagle Surveying may combine both methods when the project benefits from each.

How accurate is drone photogrammetry?+

Drone photogrammetry can produce highly accurate mapping when the flight is properly planned, imagery is clear, sufficient overlap is maintained, and the dataset is tied to appropriate ground control. Accuracy depends on flight altitude, camera quality, ground conditions, control, surface visibility, processing, and the intended deliverable. Control and quality requirements are documented in the project scope so the engineer knows exactly what the data supports.

What is the difference between LiDAR and photogrammetry?+

LiDAR measures distance with laser pulses and is particularly effective for terrain mapping, vertical detail, and ground capture through openings in vegetation. Photogrammetry reconstructs surfaces from overlapping imagery and provides detailed color and visual information. LiDAR is generally favored when bare-earth terrain is critical; photogrammetry is often favored when surfaces are visible and imagery matters. Eagle Surveying may combine both methods when the project benefits from each.

What are orthophotos?+

Orthophotos are aerial images that have been geometrically corrected for camera perspective, lens effects, and terrain displacement so they can be viewed at a consistent scale and tied to geographic coordinates. Unlike ordinary drone photographs, orthophotos can be used as measurable mapping products for existing-condition documentation, site planning, design coordination, construction records, and stakeholder review.

What is orthomosaic mapping?+

Orthomosaic mapping combines many overlapping aerial photographs into one continuous, georeferenced image of a site. The result provides a current overhead view that can be used for measurements, site planning, construction documentation, progress review, and coordination. Eagle Surveying can process orthomosaics in DroneDeploy and provide direct client access when that platform and access are included in the project scope.

Are drone surveying services FAA compliant?+

Professional UAS missions are planned and flown under applicable FAA requirements by qualified remote pilots. Before a flight, Eagle Surveying reviews airspace, operational restrictions, site conditions, weather, safety requirements, and the owner's access rules. When project requirements call for them, Eagle can deploy NDAA-compliant and Blue UAS Cleared platforms suitable for many federal, security-conscious, and critical-infrastructure environments.
